# Generative AI for Teaching and Academic Content Preparation

**Department/Role:** Computer Science Department – Teaching Assistant

## 1. Problem

As a Teaching Assistant in the Computer Science Department, preparing lectures, lab sessions, quizzes, assignments, and educational materials requires a significant amount of time and effort.

One of the main challenges is **content preparation**. Before each session, I need to collect and organize educational materials from different sources, including textbooks, documentation, research papers, tutorials, and online resources. In addition, it is important to keep the educational content up to date by following recent technologies, tools, and trends related to the topic being taught.

Another time-consuming task is preparing the **presentation itself**. Even after preparing the educational content, organizing it into a clear and engaging presentation requires considerable effort. Decisions about the structure, slide sequence, examples, diagrams, and visual explanations can take almost as much time as preparing the content itself.

The same challenge exists when preparing **quizzes and assignments**. Different versions may be required to reduce the possibility of students sharing answers. Therefore, creating multiple versions of questions while maintaining the same difficulty level and learning objectives requires additional time and manual effort.

These tasks are important for maintaining the quality of education, but a large amount of time is spent on repetitive preparation work rather than on interacting with students, explaining difficult concepts, and providing feedback.

## 2. How Generative AI Can Solve the Problem

Generative AI can act as an **academic content preparation assistant** throughout the teaching preparation process.

First, I can provide the AI with the topic, learning objectives, and the materials I have collected. The AI can help organize the information into a logical teaching structure, identify missing concepts, simplify difficult explanations, suggest examples, and generate an initial draft of the lecture content.

It can also help identify recent developments and trends related to the topic. Instead of manually reviewing many sources to determine which topics or technologies are currently relevant, Generative AI can help summarize and organize the information that has already been collected, making it easier to decide what should be included in the session.

For presentations, Generative AI can transform the prepared content into a **structured presentation plan**. For example, it can suggest the number and order of slides, slide titles, key points, examples, diagrams, and visual ideas. It can also generate detailed prompts that can be provided to another AI tool specialized in presentation generation. This significantly reduces the time spent deciding how the content should be visually presented.

Generative AI can also support **assessment preparation**. Given the topics covered in a lab or lecture, it can generate different versions of quizzes and assignments while maintaining similar difficulty levels and covering the same learning objectives. For example, it can generate multiple-choice questions, coding questions, and problem-solving questions, while changing the values, examples, or question order between versions.

However, the generated content should not be used directly without review. The Teaching Assistant remains responsible for verifying technical accuracy, educational appropriateness, difficulty level, and alignment with the course objectives.

### Why Generative AI?

Generative AI is particularly suitable for this problem because much of the preparation work involves **understanding, summarizing, restructuring, and generating natural-language content**. Traditional automation can handle repetitive formatting tasks, but Generative AI can assist with tasks that require adapting content to different teaching objectives and student levels.

The expected benefits include:

- Reducing preparation time and repetitive manual work.
- Organizing information collected from multiple sources.
- Helping keep educational materials aligned with recent trends.
- Producing clearer and more structured presentation plans.
- Generating multiple quiz and assignment versions efficiently.
- Allowing more time to focus on teaching, student interaction, and feedback.
- Supporting personalization of explanations according to students' level of understanding.

## 3. Potentially Dangerous or Sensitive Organizational Data

Although this use case mainly involves educational content, Generative AI may potentially have access to sensitive information if it is used without proper controls.

 student names, grades, or performance information in prompts could expose personally identifiable information.

## 4. Data Protection and Risk Mitigation

To use Generative AI safely, sensitive information should be minimized and protected.

Before sending information to an AI system, personally identifiable information should be removed or anonymized whenever possible. For example, student names and IDs should be replaced with anonymous identifiers.

Unpublished exams, answer keys, grades, and other confidential institutional information should not be entered into public AI models unless the institution has explicitly approved the service and appropriate data protection controls are in place.

Access should also be limited according to the principle of **least privilege**, meaning that the AI system should only receive the information required to perform the specific task.

In addition, generated content should always be reviewed by the Teaching Assistant before being shared with students. Human review is particularly important for assessment questions, technical explanations, and any content that could affect students' grades.

## Conclusion

Generative AI can significantly improve the efficiency of teaching preparation by assisting with content organization, presentation planning, trend awareness, and the generation of quizzes and assignments. The main value is not replacing the Teaching Assistant, but **reducing repetitive preparation work and allowing more time to focus on teaching and students**.

At the same time, responsible use requires protecting student information, unpublished academic materials, and other confidential institutional data. Therefore, Generative AI should be used as a controlled assistant with appropriate data protection measures and human supervision.
